Durable execution in one idea

A durable run can stop partway, for any reason, and carry on later from where it was. LangGraph gets this from checkpoints: after every step (a "super-step", in which one or more nodes run) it saves the whole state under a thread ID. Give each claim its own thread, keep the checkpoints in Postgres, and any process anywhere can pick a claim up by its ID.

The vocabulary: the state is a typed dictionary; nodes are functions that return updates to it; edges are fixed or conditional; reducers say how updates combine (such as appending messages).

durability="sync" writes each checkpoint before the next step starts, so a crash can't lose a step that already happened. It's slower than the default, and right for anything involving money.

Never pay twice

Two facts about resuming catch people out. After a crash, the node that was running starts again from the top. And when a graph resumes after interrupt(), the node that paused also re-runs from its start, with interrupt() now returning the human's answer. So any side effect placed before an interrupt(), or in a node that can crash halfway, can happen twice. The defenses:

  • Put each side effect in a node of its own, after the pause, never before it.
  • Make it idempotent: here the claim ID is the payments table's primary key, so a second insert does nothing.
  • Remember that rewinding to an earlier checkpoint rewinds the graph's state, not the world. Undoing a payment takes a business action, such as a refund.

Designing the human's step

  • When to ask. Only where a person adds something: above a money limit, on low confidence, on anything irreversible. Ask too often and people approve without reading (decision fatigue).
  • What to show. The decision, the amount, the rationale with the policy section, and the evidence, on one screen, so the handler can check rather than redo the work.
  • What to record. Who signed off, when, and anything they changed or noted, so the decision is auditable later.

Takeaways

  • Checkpoints per step on a thread per claim make runs durable across restarts and machines.
  • interrupt() pauses for a person; Command(resume=...) carries on, from any process.
  • Nodes re-run after crashes and resumes, so side effects go in their own idempotent nodes.
  • Ask people only where they add value, show them everything on one screen, and record what they decided.

Check your understanding

A node calls pay() and then interrupt() to ask the handler for a note. The handler resumes it. What happens?

Code before interrupt() runs again when the node resumes. Side effects belong after the pause, in their own node.

What makes the payment idempotent in the example?

Durability saves progress; idempotency makes repeating an action harmless. You need both.

You rewind a claim's thread to a checkpoint before the payment. Is the money returned?

Time travel applies to the agent's state, not to side effects already made.
